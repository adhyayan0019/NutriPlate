Abstract—Automated dietary assessment is very critical in
reducing the worrying rise of non-communicable diseases such
as Type-2 diabetes and cardiovascular conditions in South Asia.
The existing computer vision based dietary assessment systems
however are completely incompetent in processing Indian dietary
scenarios given the lack of defined boundaries in liquid-based
foods, high intra-class variability in images, composite food shapes,
and severe object occlusions in a thali setup. In this paper,
we present NutriPlate, which is an innovative, decoupled edge-
cloud system tailored to perform real-time automated Indian
dietary analysis. The key ingredient of the system is an extremely
lightweight anchor-free instance segmentation network with a
unique Prototypical mask synthesis head that enables pixel-perfect
instance segmentation in 24.8 ms on mobile devices. Segmented
objects are then calibrated using a reference plate and mapped
to volumes and weights of food portions. Most importantly,
NutriPlate establishes the semantics between visual food analysis
and metabolic health by means of a biochemical interaction
matrix based on ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 and DGI 2024. This
engine captures non-linear micronutrient synergy (such as ascorbic
acid-mediated non-heme iron reduction) and chelation chemistry
(phytic acid and tannin-mediated precipitation of iron/zinc) of
the antagonists. Also, the active learning system with the 576-
dimensional visual gallery of food objects helps in zero-training
adaptation to user-corrected food objects. NutriPlate provides a
benchmarking score of 89.4% mAP@50, 82.7% mask IoU, and a
portion estimation error of 8.8% across 412 Indian dishes.
Index Terms—Deep Instance Segmentation, Edge-Cloud Com-
puting, Dietary Assessment, Indian Food Composition (IFCT),
Biochemical Synergy, Monocular Volumetric Estimation, Active
Learning.
I. INTRODUCTION
Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) like T2DM (Type-2
Diabetes Mellitus) and cardiovascular diseases have become
epidemic in South Asia, with India being called the diabetes
capital of the world [1]. Modification of diet is the key
strategy to manage metabolic syndrome disorders [2]. However,
conventional approaches for recording dietary intake of patients,
such as 24 hour dietary recall (24HDR) and self-reported food
diaries, are prone to huge recall bias, reporting underestimations
of 30-40% and fast patient compliance [3].
Even though there has been growing popularity in visual food
recognition through smartphones, the current methodologies
are solely based on benchmarks from the West, namely Food-
101 [4] and Nutrition5k [5]. Such techniques break down when
faced with traditional Indian meals owing to the following four
computational problems:
1) Viscosity and Overlapping Components:Unlike West-
ern food items which have a clear separation between
different ingredients (e.g. burger or an apple), Indian food
items have highly viscous gravies such as sambars, dals
and kormas, vegetable stir-fry (sabzi) and overlapping
flatbreads like roti and paratha.
2) High Intra-class and Inter-class Visual Ambiguity:
Foods that display similar visual color spectrums (such
as yellowdal tadkaand yellowkadhi pakora) exhibit
vastly different macronutrient and glycemic indexes.
3) Neglect of Biochemical Bioavailability:Standard calorie
counting systems rely on an oversimplistic 1:1 relation of
consumption-to-absorption of nutritional intake. The truth
is that classical Indian plant-based foods have antinutri-
tional chelators like phytates, polyphenolic tannins which
bind essential divalent cations like Fe2+,Zn 2+,Ca 2+ and
thus decrease the bioavailable absorption up to 70%
unless neutralized by organic acid promoters, for instance
ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) [6], [7].
4) Inference Latency and Heat Issues:Complex two-step
instance segmentation models such as Mask R-CNN
[8] use more than 120 GFLOPs and thus experience a
considerable inference latency (>300ms).
To overcome these fundamental bottlenecks, this paper
introducesNutriPlate, an edge-cloud decoupled framework for
granular Indian dietary assessment. Our primary contributions
are:
• An anchor-free architecture for edge optimization with
ProtoNet prototype mask generation, which produces sub-
25 ms mobile inference time with 89.4% mAP@50 on
412 Indian food categories.
• A formulation for monocular estimation of volume por-
tions based on calibration of dining plate edges and food
shape features (ξ i).

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Computer Vision in Dietary Assessment
Early visual dietary assessment systems regarded food
tracking as an elementary image classification problem [4], [9].
Despite obtaining impressive top-5 performance for classifi-
cation of individual dishes on plates by deep convolutional
backbone networks and ViT [10], the approaches fail entirely
when it comes to plates containing multiple dishes in the case
of Indian thali. Multi-label classification algorithms handle the
co-occurrence of dishes, but not their pixel-wise masks.
B. Real-Time Instance Segmentation
Two-stage approaches like Mask R-CNN [8] provide good
localization through Region Proposal Network (RPN) and
RoI-Align operations; however, their substantial compute
burden makes them unsuitable for real-time inference on
mobile devices. On the other hand, single-stage detectors
[12], [13] and prototypical instance segmentation approaches,
such as YOLACT [14] and YOLOv8-seg [15], separate mask
prediction into prototype base prediction across the entire image
and instance coefficients. NutriPlate follows the prototypical
approach with added specular reflection removal and culinary
color texture heuristics.
C. Portion Estimation and Nutritional Knowledge Bases
In the past, portion estimation needed to make use of
binocular stereo cameras, structured light sensors for depth, or

into account the cooking processes in India.

IV. EXPERIMENTALSETUP ANDRESULTS
A. Dataset Taxonomy and Benchmark Baselines
We benchmarked NutriPlate on 20,470 Indian meal images
with annotations belonging to 412 food classes across 5 regions
in India. Table I depicts the dataset taxonomy. NutriPlate uses
an ultra-light anchor-free ProtoNet segmentation model (with
3.2M parameters, based on the YOLOv8-seg Nano network
architecture) that was fine-tuned for 412 Indian food classes for
the edge client. Table II provides benchmark results compared
to state-of-the-art detection and segmentation models, two-stage
Mask R-CNN, and a heavy cloud-based baseline YOLOv8x-seg
(with 71.8M parameters).
B. Training Dynamics and Empirical Convergence
The anchor-free ProtoNet segmentation model, NutriPlate,
was trained for 200 epochs under the multi-task loss criterion, as
given by Eq. (6). The accuracy evolution with respect to training
and validation data sets is demonstrated by Fig. 4. Accuracy
on the training data set rises sharply up to 25 epochs and
reaches 97.44%. Meanwhile, accuracy on the testing data set
saturates at 91.95% by epoch 195. This small generalization gap
(≈6.2% ) indicates the effectiveness of mosaic augmentations
and distribution focal loss to avoid overfitting.
Fig. 5. Convergence dynamics of cross-entropy loss through 200 training
epochs. Monotonic stabilization of training and validation loss ensures a smooth
gradient descent without any numerical issues.
Figure 5 depicts the respective loss curve in terms of cross-
entropy for 200 epochs. For both training and validation loss
curves, we have seen very sharp declines initially, falling
below 0.50 at epoch 75 and reaching asymptotes of 0.10 and
0.32, respectively. This fact is further evident from the lack
of diverging validation loss, which ensures proper gradient
descent and robust feature learning for all types of curry, rice,
and reflective steel textures.
C. Benchmark Performance and Edge Efficiency
Based on results presented in Table II, NutriPlate provides
significant efficiency over two-stage and heavy server-grade
benchmarks. Although ResNet50-Mask R-CNN takes 186.4 ms,
NutriPlate reduces the inference time down to24.8 ms( 7.5×
speedup) with 92.7% parameters’ compression. Though server-
grade YOLOv8x-seg provides slightly higher bounding boxes
mAP ( 91.3%), due to 71.8M parameters and 344.1 GFLOP
computations, it suffers from excessive heat throttling, making
the frame rate below 4 FPS. NutriPlate is the unique model
among all investigated segmentation models that operates
within the real-time operating range ( >30 FPS ↔<33.3 ms)
with 87.2% mask mAP@50 and 82.7% mask IoU scores for
difficult Indian cuisines (AUC=0.92 Roti, AUC=0.87 Curry,
AUC=0.89 Rice).

V. DISCUSSION ANDPRACTICALDEPLOYMENT
Deploying deep learning in daily dietary practice presents
several domain-specific edge complexities:
1) Penetration of Liquid Gravy and Coexistence:Liquid
curries tend to seep into the rice piles. In this regard, the
ProtoNet head is able to tackle this problem effectively
by generating smooth spatial masks rather than hard
boxes.
2) Edge Client Battery Consumption and Heat Genera-
tion:Through offloading intensive matrix operations and
asynchronous learning on the cloud via microservices,
the edge client requires just 420 mW average energy for
inference tasks, saving mobile battery power.
3) Clinical Impact on Glycemic Control in South Asians:
The traditional dietary practices of Indians often result
in rapid postprandial glucose spikes due to high amounts
of refined carbohydrates and insufficient fiber. Through
its meal scoring rules, NutriPlate allows the patient to
lower the bedtime glucose spike.
VI. CONCLUSION ANDFUTURESCOPE
NutriPlateis the clinical, decoupled framework proposed
in this research for Indian diet analysis. Utilizing anchor-free
prototypical instance segmentation, monocular volume com-
putation, ICMR-NIN IFCT data, and Biochemical Interaction
Matrix, NutriPlate addresses the problem of non-conventional
food boundary and bioavailability functions. NutriPlate obtains
89.4% mAP@50 accuracy with 24.8 ms inference time and
portion estimation error of 8.8%. The proposed system provides
a scalable basis for the prevention of diabetes. We plan
to explore the usage of ToF cameras in combination with
continuous glucose monitoring telemetry in the future research
work.
